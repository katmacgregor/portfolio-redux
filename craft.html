a History ofMagic

Four years ago, I combined my love of art, décor, and storytelling into recreating props and sets inspired by the Harry Potter series. I set to work creating flying keys, custom potions, and a light-activated screaming mandrake. Months of crafting culminated in a themed party, complete with sorting and a competition for the House Cup.

Following the first year's success I decided to host the event regularly. The second year, I decided to start following the cadence of each book so each year has its own theme, props, settings, and activities inspired by the story.

Transfiguration

Each year has introduced new mediums and technologies to explore like sculpture, bookbinding, and microcontrollers. I love having an idea and creating sketches and paper prototypes as I scale it into something real.

I map out some ideas in the beginning, like the Knight Bus, but others come together as I make them up, like the Shrieking Shack. The most interesting part is finding new ways to solve puzzles with each piece and project.

Care of Magical Details

I have an obsession for detail and spend hours on little touches, hoping that someone gets a moment of delight when they find it.

Usually this means 2 or 3 people find these details on their own, and tend to spend the rest of the party showing everyone else. These playful moments create a sense of seek and reward, and let people have a deeper interaction with the environment.

Muggle Studies

Everyone is sorted into a Hogwarts house their first year. Newbies are welcomed by previously sorted attendees, and I've seen new friendships form and grow each year.

Since no party is complete without unnecessarily complex competition, I created elaborate scavenger hunts where each clue and riddle leads to the next, and every year turns into a little case study: what do people enjoy, what do they notice, and what can I improve? I scaled the activities for various trivia knowledge levels, and hide answers in the decor so that someone unfamiliar could still help solve the clues.

Charms

This has become an outlet outside of work with no client, requirements, and definitely no practical reason. I get to follow an idea because I’m curious about it, learn something new because I want to, and make something simply because I think it would be fun.

I compete with myself each year, wanting to outdo the previous year, enhance what I've already created, and keep exploring and learning.

Prepping for Year 4, I'm following the book's lead to detour from the House Cup to a Triwizard Tournament, complete with a Yule Ball theme. I can't wait to share what rabbit hole I've gone down next.
