Star Wars

02 / Web Application Developer · 2015

I worked on StarWars.com as one of 3 front-end developers responsible for building and launching the site in just a few months, using Backbone, Mustache, Sass, and Sinatra.

I leveraged my background in design to develop new modules, data models, and reusable templates, including the Star Wars Databank, while keeping the codebase lean as the number of modules and content types grew. Design informed how I structured flexible code to support future customization, while understanding the underlying technology allowed me to push designs beyond static compositions into interactions that were both feasible and dynamic.

Building the Foundation

At the time, many interactive and franchise websites still relied heavily on Photoshop and Flash for their visual and interactive experiences. StarWars.com was part of a shift within Disney toward performant, HTML5-based experiences that could deliver the same level of visual expression without relying on those technologies.

The site needed a layout that could mix content of very different shapes, from video and articles to ads, side by side, like a magazine spread. It also had to support Internet Explorer 9, which had no CSS Grid or Flexbox support. We built a modular tile grid that ran across the entire site, allowing stylized modules to adapt fluidly across screen sizes while degrading gracefully on older browsers. This let us preserve the visual character of the design without sacrificing responsiveness or browser support.

Interaction Details

Even the smallest interactions were opportunities to extend the story for our users. I designed and built the mobile menu so the hamburger icon transforms into glowing lightsabers when opened, turning a functional control into a small moment of delight. The interaction was later featured by Little Big Details and designmodo.

Theming at Scale

To make the site's distinctive notched, dark-gray containers honor the design intent without sacrificng performance, I built configurable Sass mixins that scaled the same visual language across the site's many modules. This allowed the visual system to remain consistent while giving each module enough flexibility to support different content and layouts.

Takeaways

The launch work earned me a promotion to Senior Software Engineer and established a pattern I still use today: build a strong foundation that can expand, so efficiency creates more room for the details that elevate the experience. The configurable mixins, shared grid, and lightsaber toggle were all expressions of that approach. As a design leader, I carry that thinking into how I approach systems and processes, using structure to protect the quality and craft of the experience.
